# Feature Specification: Adaptive Assessment and Real-Time Scoring

**Feature Branch**: `013-adaptive-assessment-scoring`

**Created**: 2026-09-30

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "A self-evaluating wizard that determines a student's level 0-10 via progressively-difficult questions, updates the score in real time as they use the platform, uses a downgrade as a tool to ease the path for struggling students, and keeps a rock-solid activity log — formalized from the research and proof-of-concept at `workshop/docs/research/education-platform/adaptive-assessment-and-scoring.md`."

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - A learner's ability score updates in real time from practice-bank answers (Priority: P1)

A learner works through the open practice question bank for an area. Today, answering a practice question produces no server-side record at all — there is no endpoint to submit a practice response, so nothing is graded, logged, or scored, and there is no mechanism to track "level" across a session. This story delivers the foundational capability: a learner submits an answer to a practice question, the system records that response as an immutable log entry, computes an updated 0-10 ability estimate using an Elo-style update (their score moving up after a correct answer on a hard-for-them item, down after an incorrect answer on an easy-for-them item), and the next question served is chosen near the learner's current estimate.

**Why this priority**: This is the foundation every other story depends on. Without a submit endpoint and a response log, there is nothing to update in real time (Story 2) and no data to trigger a downgrade (also Story 2) or later recalibrate against (Story 3). It is also independently valuable and independently testable on its own: a learner can get a working, adapting practice session with only this story built.

**Independent Test**: Submit a sequence of answers (mixed correct/incorrect) for one session against one area's practice bank via the new submit endpoint. Confirm each submission creates one new, immutable response-log row; confirm the session's current ability score changes after each submission in the mathematically expected direction and magnitude; confirm the next question served by the practice-bank endpoint is chosen near the updated estimate rather than being unrelated to it.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a learner with no prior response history in an area (cold start, ability estimate at the 5.0 scale midpoint), **When** they submit a correct answer to a practice question, **Then** their ability estimate increases by an amount reflecting a larger, cold-start-sized update step than a learner with many prior attempts would receive for the same result.
2. **Given** a learner's current ability estimate and a specific question's served difficulty, **When** they submit an answer, **Then** the system records one new `ResponseLogEntry` capturing the served difficulty, the ability estimate immediately before the update, the ability estimate immediately after, the K-factor (update step size) used, whether the answer was correct, and a human-readable reason string — and this entry is never later modified or deleted.
3. **Given** a learner has just submitted an answer, **When** they immediately request their current score, **Then** the returned score reflects the just-submitted answer's effect (no staleness, no waiting on a batch process).
4. **Given** the existing gated end-of-area assessment (`AssessmentSubmitHandler`) is submitted, **When** its per-question outcomes are produced, **Then** those outcomes feed the same response-log and ability-score update pipeline as open-practice-bank submissions, rather than being a second, disconnected scoring path.

### User Story 2 - A struggling learner is automatically routed to easier content (Priority: P2)

A learner who answers several practice questions incorrectly in a row is, today, simply served the next nearest-to-ability question regardless of how demoralizing the streak has been — there is no mechanism that treats a downgrade as anything other than a lower number. This story makes a downgrade change what the system actually serves next: after a defined run of consecutive wrong answers, the next question is deliberately served at a measurably easier difficulty than the normal adaptive pick, giving the learner a chance to rebuild a run of correct answers before difficulty climbs again. This story is the scoring-and-routing half of the real-time UI integration; the UI/gamification presentation of that score (badges, toasts, visual score displays) is covered by the companion `014-gamification-design-system` feature and is explicitly out of scope here — this story defines what the server does and what it exposes, not how it is displayed.

**Why this priority**: This is the literal behavior the operator asked for — "a downgrade as a tool to ease the path," not a punitive number — and it is the first behavior that makes the ability score do something beyond bookkeeping. It depends on Story 1's submit endpoint and response log already existing (a downgrade trigger is evaluated against the just-recorded response history), which is why it is not P1.

**Independent Test**: Submit three consecutive incorrect answers for one learner in one area via the Story 1 submit endpoint. Confirm the fourth question served is measurably easier than the question that would have been served by the normal nearest-to-ability selection rule, and confirm the triggering response-log entry (or a linked downgrade event) carries an explicit reason string naming the trigger condition. Confirm a subsequent correct answer resumes normal nearest-to-ability selection.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a learner's three most recent response-log entries in an area are all incorrect, **When** they request or are served their next practice question, **Then** the served question's difficulty is measurably lower than the difficulty the normal nearest-to-ability rule would have selected, and the response-log entry recording their next submission has `DowngradeTriggered` set with a reason string naming the trigger.
2. **Given** a downgrade has just been triggered, **When** the learner answers the easier question correctly, **Then** the system resumes normal nearest-to-ability question selection on the following question (the downgrade is a temporary routing change, not a persistent difficulty ceiling).
3. **Given** `GET /api/progress` already reports lesson-completion status for a session, **When** this story ships, **Then** that same endpoint also reports the session's current `AbilityScore`, so a UI-facing feature (014) has one progress surface to read rather than a second one to separately poll.
4. **Given** a learner's ability score changes, **When** a client observes it, **Then** the change and its cause are available to the client as data (score, reason, whether a downgrade occurred) — this story defines that data contract only; rendering it is out of scope here and is `014-gamification-design-system`'s responsibility.

### User Story 3 - Item difficulty is crowd-calibrated from real response data, and later recalibrated toward statistically rigorous estimates (Priority: P3)

Today, every question's difficulty is a static, author-assigned label (`easy`/`medium`/`hard`) that is carried through as descriptive metadata only and never adjusted from real usage. This story makes item difficulty a live value that drifts based on the accumulated pool of real learner responses (the "crowd calibration" behavior), recomputed in a periodic batch process rather than on every single response, so that an item repeatedly found easier or harder than its author's label suggests self-corrects over time. Once sufficient response-log volume exists, this story's data is also what lets a future upgrade path evaluate fitting statistically rigorous per-item parameters (the kind of calibration real Computerized Adaptive Testing platforms use), graduating high-volume items off the bootstrap scheme while leaving freshly authored items on it.

**Why this priority**: This is explicitly the long-term refinement, not the foundation. Stories 1 and 2 are fully functional and independently valuable using only static, author-seeded difficulty values; this story is what keeps those values honest over time and is the graduation path toward deeper statistical rigor. It requires real response volume from Stories 1-2 already being collected before it can do anything meaningful.

**Independent Test**: Accumulate a batch of response-log entries against one question (mixing correct and incorrect outcomes from learners whose ability estimates bracket the question's seeded difficulty). Run the batch recalibration process. Confirm the question's `ItemDifficultyState.DifficultyEstimate` has moved from its seed value in the direction the aggregate response pattern implies (down if learners generally answered it correctly more often than their ability would predict, up if they generally missed it), and confirm the question's live-serving difficulty used for subsequent item selection reflects the updated estimate rather than the original seed.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a question seeded at a given difficulty from its author label, **When** enough real responses accumulate against it and the batch recalibration process runs, **Then** its difficulty estimate moves toward what the aggregate response pattern implies, with the update rate shrinking as the question's own response count grows (an item with many responses moves only slightly on each additional one).
2. **Given** the response log has accumulated substantial real volume for a specific question, **When** an operator or downstream process evaluates that question for a statistically rigorous (calibrated-parameter) treatment, **Then** the accumulated response-log history for that question is sufficient input to attempt that evaluation without a separate, from-scratch data-collection effort.
3. **Given** item-difficulty recalibration runs as a batch process rather than synchronously, **When** a learner submits a single response, **Then** that submission's own latency and correctness are unaffected by whether a batch recalibration is concurrently running.

---

### Edge Cases

- **Cold start with zero pre-seeded "easy" items.** The current practice bank contains 311 questions with a `difficulty` label populated on all of them, but the distribution is 121 `medium` / 190 `hard` / **0 `easy`** — so a genuinely new learner has no item pre-seeded below the scale midpoint to start on. The system's cold-start behavior (always serving a low-difficulty first item) must have a real item to serve; where none is authored-labeled `easy`, the cold-start item selection MUST fall back to an alternative source (e.g. the graded catalog, which carries no difficulty label and seeds at the scale midpoint) rather than silently serving a `medium`-or-harder item as if it were easy.
- **Cold start for a newly authored item.** A question with no authored difficulty label at all (true of every item in the `curriculum-kit` graded catalog) seeds at the scale midpoint (5.0) as an explicit "unknown, to be calibrated" default — it must never be silently treated as `medium`.
- **Cold start for a newly enrolled learner.** For the first several responses in a session, the ability estimate is running on a cold-start prior with essentially zero evidence behind it. The system must expose enough information (e.g. an attempt count) for a caller to distinguish an early, low-confidence estimate from a later, better-converged one — a score must never be presented with the same implied confidence regardless of how many responses produced it.
- **Repeated attempts at the same question.** Nothing in this feature's core update rule prevents a learner from re-submitting the same question multiple times to inflate their score; a repeat-attempt/gaming detector is explicitly out of scope for this feature (deferred to a later phase) but the append-only response log MUST retain enough detail (session, question, timestamp) that such a detector could be built later without a schema change.
- **Interrupted downgrade streak.** A learner whose last three attempts are wrong-wrong-**correct**-wrong has not met a "3 consecutive wrong" trigger at that point (the streak was broken by the correct answer) — the downgrade trigger condition MUST be evaluated against a genuinely consecutive run, not merely "3 of the last N."
- **Ability estimate at the scale boundary.** An extremely strong or extremely struggling learner's estimate could mathematically be pushed outside the 0-10 range by repeated one-directional updates; the estimate MUST be clamped to the documented [0, 10] range on every update.
- **Item-difficulty batch lag.** Because item-difficulty recalibration is batched rather than synchronous, a learner's item selection between batch runs is made against a possibly-stale difficulty estimate; this is an accepted, documented tradeoff (§3.4 of the source research) rather than a defect, and the served difficulty actually used MUST still be the one logged on the resulting `ResponseLogEntry`, even though it may differ from the item's current (not-yet-batch-updated) difficulty state.
- **Graded assessment and open practice interacting on the same question.** Because the same authored content is exposed both as an open-practice-bank question and (for a subset) as a graded end-of-area question, a learner's response through either surface MUST feed the same shared response-log and ability-update pipeline (User Story 1, Acceptance Scenario 4) rather than producing two disconnected histories for what is, to the learner, one topic.
- **Duplicate or replayed submission.** A submission for a question the learner has not been served, or a malformed/replayed submission, MUST be refused at the boundary (fail closed) rather than silently scored in a way that could benefit the learner's estimate, matching the existing fail-closed pattern already used by this platform's answer-disclosure logic.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: The system MUST provide a submit endpoint for the open practice question bank that accepts a learner's answer to a specific question and records the outcome; no such endpoint exists today (only a one-shot, lesson-gated end-of-area assessment submit route exists), so this is a new capability, not a modification of an existing route.
- **FR-002**: The system MUST record every scored response — from the new practice-bank submit endpoint and from the existing graded end-of-area assessment submit endpoint alike — as a single immutable `ResponseLogEntry`, including at minimum: the session identity, the question identity, the timestamp, whether the response was correct, the difficulty as served, the ability estimate immediately before and immediately after the update, the update step size (K-factor) applied, and a human-readable reason string describing why that update occurred.
- **FR-003**: A `ResponseLogEntry`, once written, MUST NOT be mutated or deleted. A correction to a previously logged score MUST be made by writing a new, compensating entry with its own reason, never by editing history.
- **FR-004**: The system MUST maintain a current ability estimate on a 0-10 scale (10 = strongest) per learner session, updated synchronously on the same request that scores a response, such that a learner who checks their score immediately after answering sees the updated value with no dependency on a batch process.
- **FR-005**: The ability-update calculation MUST use a smaller update step size (K-factor) for a learner with more prior recorded attempts than for a learner with fewer, so that early responses move the estimate more than later ones (decaying-step-size behavior).
- **FR-006**: The ability estimate MUST be clamped to the [0, 10] range on every update, regardless of the magnitude or direction of the computed update.
- **FR-007**: The current ability estimate exposed for a session MUST carry a link back to the specific `ResponseLogEntry` that most recently produced it, so that any current score is traceable to exactly one logged cause with no ambiguity.
- **FR-008**: Every question's item difficulty MUST be seeded from its existing authored difficulty label on the same 0-10 scale used for ability, using a documented mapping (e.g. `easy`→low, `medium`→mid, `hard`→high); a question carrying no difficulty label at all MUST seed at the scale midpoint as an explicit "uncalibrated" default rather than being silently treated as `medium`.
- **FR-009**: Because the current practice bank contains zero questions labeled `easy` (121 `medium`, 190 `hard`, 0 `easy` of 311 total), the system's cold-start item-selection behavior MUST have a defined fallback source for a genuinely low-difficulty first item (e.g. drawing from the graded catalog, whose items carry no difficulty label and seed at the midpoint) rather than serving a `medium`-or-harder item to a brand-new learner.
- **FR-010**: Item difficulty estimates MUST be recalibrated from accumulated real response data in a periodic batch process (not synchronously on every individual response), using an update step size that shrinks as a given item accumulates more responses, so that a single response does not meaningfully move an item's difficulty once it has substantial response history.
- **FR-011**: When a learner's most recent consecutive responses in an area meet a defined "struggling" trigger condition (a run of consecutive incorrect answers), the system MUST serve their next question at a difficulty measurably below what normal nearest-to-ability selection would otherwise pick, and the response-log entry for that downgrade-triggered attempt MUST record `DowngradeTriggered = true` with an explicit, human-readable reason string naming the trigger condition.
- **FR-012**: A downgrade MUST be a temporary routing adjustment, not a persistent ceiling: once the triggering streak is broken by a correct answer, subsequent item selection MUST resume normal nearest-to-ability behavior.
- **FR-013**: The system MUST expose the current ability score (and, for a downgrade event, its reason) through the existing session progress read surface (`GET /api/progress`) rather than introducing a separate, independently-polled progress endpoint, so a consuming UI has one progress surface to read.
- **FR-014**: A submission for a question the requesting session was not actually served, or a structurally malformed/replayed submission, MUST be refused at the request boundary rather than silently scored, and MUST NOT default toward an outcome that benefits the learner's score when the request cannot be validated.
- **FR-015**: The response log MUST retain enough per-entry detail (session, question, timestamp, outcome) that a later feature could detect repeated attempts at the same question by the same session without requiring a schema change to the log itself; building that detector is explicitly out of scope for this feature.
- **FR-016**: The exposed ability estimate for a session MUST be accompanied by a count of attempts that produced it (or equivalent confidence signal), so that a consuming caller can distinguish a low-evidence, early-session estimate from a better-converged one rather than treating every score as equally certain.

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **ResponseLogEntry**: The append-only audit record of one scored activity, and the sole source of truth every derived ability and item-difficulty number is computed from. Carries the session and question identity, timestamp, correctness, the difficulty as served (captured at the moment it was served, not re-derived later even if the item's difficulty later drifts), the ability estimate immediately before and after, the K-factor used, and — when applicable — a downgrade flag and its reason. Never mutated or deleted after being written; a correction is a new entry, never an edit.
- **AbilityScore**: The current, fast-read 0-10 ability value for a session (optionally scoped per curriculum area — see Clarification 1 below), kept separate from the response log so that "what is my score right now" is a direct read rather than a scan-and-fold over the full log on every request. Carries an attempt count and a link to the `ResponseLogEntry` that most recently produced it (its audit link).
- **ItemDifficultyState**: The crowd-calibrated difficulty side of a question — its original authored-label seed, its current (batch-updated) difficulty estimate, and the count of responses that have contributed to that estimate. Updated only by the periodic batch process, never synchronously in the request path that scores a single learner's answer.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: 100% of submitted practice-bank answers produce exactly one new, immutable response-log entry — where today 0% of practice-bank answers produce any server-side record at all.
- **SC-002**: A learner's ability score reflects their most recent submitted answer on the very next read of that score, with no observable delay or dependency on a batch process, verified across repeated submit-then-read sequences.
- **SC-003**: For a learner whose simulated response pattern matches a consistent underlying skill level, the ability estimate's absolute error against that underlying level narrows as attempts accumulate and stabilizes to within a small, bounded range after roughly 30 attempts — consistent with the proof-of-concept's own captured results (three simulated learners converging to within 0.17-0.43 points of their hidden true ability over 30 attempts, on the scale's 0-10 range).
- **SC-004**: 100% of downgrade-triggering conditions (a defined run of consecutive incorrect answers) result in the very next served question being measurably easier than the normal nearest-to-ability pick, and 100% of downgrade events carry an explicit, non-empty reason string in their response-log entry.
- **SC-005**: 100% of ability-score values readable at any point in time are traceable, via their recorded audit link, to exactly one specific logged response — an auditor can always answer "why is the score this value" with a specific entry and cause, never a guess.
- **SC-006**: Zero response-log entries are ever mutated or deleted after being written, verified by the storage mechanism's own constraints (append-only) rather than by application-level discipline alone.
- **SC-007**: A question with substantial accumulated response volume shows its item-difficulty update step measurably smaller than a question with little response volume, for a comparably-sized batch of new responses (the crowd-calibration update rate genuinely shrinks with volume, not just in documentation).
- **SC-008**: A caller reading a session's ability score can always also obtain that score's attempt count, so early-session, low-evidence scores are distinguishable from later, better-converged ones in every case, not just some.

## Assumptions

- The existing SQLite-backed storage pattern already used in this codebase (`pkg/crossref`, via `modernc.org/sqlite`) is an acceptable, sufficient storage mechanism for the new `ResponseLogEntry`, `AbilityScore`, and `ItemDifficultyState` data — no new storage technology needs to be introduced for this feature.
- The Elo-style update approach (student ability and item difficulty as two sides of a match, per Klinkenberg et al. 2011's Math Garden precedent) is the right approach for this platform's current scale (311 questions, zero calibrated parameters) rather than full Item Response Theory; a future graduation to IRT-style calibration for high-volume items is explicitly the subject of User Story 3 / Phase 3, not this feature's Phase 1 scope.
- The existing `citations_study_href` / `lesson_sections_study_href` fields already served by the practice-bank questions endpoint are an acceptable existing hook for a future remedial-material-routing downgrade behavior; wiring a downgrade event to proactively surface that link, and widening downgrade routing to an easier question *kind* (e.g. flashcard instead of free-response), are real mechanisms named in the source research but are deferred to the `014-gamification-design-system` UI-integration work or a later iteration of this feature, not required by this feature's Phase 1 scope.
- This feature's Phase 1 scope (User Story 1, and the server-side routing half of User Story 2) does not require or depend on any UI, badge, toast, or other visual presentation of the score — that presentation layer is the explicit responsibility of the companion `014-gamification-design-system` feature, which this feature exposes a stable data contract for (current score, attempt count, downgrade reason) but does not itself build.
- The existing `curriculum-kit` graded end-of-area assessment's own disclosure/answer-withholding logic (`GradedPromptIndex`, `AssessmentSubmitHandler`) is unaffected by this feature; this feature adds a new write path into the shared scoring pipeline for that assessment's outcomes, it does not change how or whether an answer key is disclosed.
- A repeat-attempt/gaming detector, and a migration to statistically calibrated (IRT-style) item parameters, are real, named future work (Phase 3 / User Story 3's upper bound) that this feature's data model must not block, but building either is out of scope for this feature itself.

## Clarifications Needed

- **[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: Per-area ability score vs. single cross-curriculum number]** — The source research explicitly flags this as an open operator decision that must be made before Phase 2 (UI integration) locks in a stable score shape: should a learner have one ability estimate per curriculum area (so they can be "strong" in one subject and "struggling" in another, matching how the curriculum is organized into areas), or a single cross-curriculum number? The `AbilityScore` entity's optional per-area scoping is designed to support either without a schema change, but which one actually drives item selection and is shown to the learner is undecided. This decision should be made before `014-gamification-design-system` UI work commits to a specific score display.
- **[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: Update-step-size (K-factor) and logistic-divisor tuning constants]** — The proof-of-concept's constants (cold-start step size, steady-state step size, the decay rate between them, and the logistic-response divisor) were chosen for demonstration purposes and documented as such in the source research; they were not fit against any real workshop-platform response data, because no such data exists yet (this feature is what will start generating it). A production rollout needs to monitor real convergence behavior against these starting values and be prepared to retune them — this feature should treat the specific numeric constants as configurable rather than hardcoded, but the actual production-validated values are not yet known and cannot be specified here.
- **[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: Downgrade trigger threshold and downgrade magnitude]** — The proof-of-concept demonstrates a specific downgrade rule (a run of 3 consecutive incorrect answers triggers serving the next item at a fixed number of scale-points easier than the normal pick), and it produces sensible-looking behavior in the demonstration — but like the K-factor constants above, this specific threshold and magnitude were chosen for the demonstration, not validated against real learner outcomes. Whether 3-in-a-row is the right trigger (versus, say, 2 or 4) and whether a fixed point-value reduction is the right downgrade shape (versus a percentage, or a tier drop) is an open tuning question.
- **[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: Item-difficulty batch recalibration cadence]** — The source research proposes "hourly, or after N new responses, whichever is sooner" as a starting cadence for the batch recalibration job, explicitly as a proposal rather than a decision. The actual cadence affects how quickly item difficulty self-corrects versus how much load the batch job places on the system, and needs an explicit choice.
- **[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: Content-authoring response to the zero-`easy`-items gap]** — Beyond this feature's required code-level fallback (FR-009), should curriculum authors be asked to retroactively label some existing questions `easy`, should new `easy`-tier questions be authored, or is the graded-catalog fallback judged sufficient long-term? This is a content/operator decision the source research names but does not resolve, and it is separate from (though related to) the per-area-vs-cross-curriculum decision above.
